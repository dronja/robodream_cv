# Model card: facehall reference models

> The two models that `facehall enhance`, `facehall serve` and the Docker images use by default
> (`configs/pipeline/default.yaml`). Last updated: **2026-11-27**.
>
> - Every number below is from a real run. The tables are copied from
>   [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md) (generated by `scripts/make_results.py`) and from
>   [`PROJECT.md`](PROJECT.md) §9; the reasons are in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md).
> - Unverified points are marked *needs verification*.
> - The weights are not published (D39).

Model versions and checksums: PR 6.3.

## 2. Intended use

- **Restoring small faces** in photos and video frames, offline and locally (D1, D13):
  - ×4 for faces with about 8 px between the eyes;
  - ×8 for faces with about 4 px between the eyes.
- **A viewing aid:** a plausible, sharper version of a face, shown next to the original.
- **Non-commercial research only** (D2), because of the training data and the third-party models
  used in training (§9).

## 3. Out of scope

- **Identifying a person,** verifying an identity, or comparing faces with a face-recognition
  system. The output is a reconstruction: its details come from the training faces as much as
  from the input (`CLAUDE.md` §11).
- **Evidence** of any kind, in investigations or elsewhere. At most a hint, with the original
  next to it.
- **Commercial use,** a public service, or a published image or weights (D2, D13, D39).
- **Faces outside the size range:** a face more than twice as large or as small as the model
  input is left to the bicubic background by the pipeline.
- **Making a face look like a specific person,** or generating faces from nothing. Given a box
  without a face (`--box`), the model still draws a face there.

## 4. How the models are used

`facehall.pipeline` runs, for each photo or video frame:

1. **Detection:** YuNet (OpenCV Zoo, D31) on a bicubic upscale by the model scale, score
   threshold 0.8 (D33). A face box given by the user is the fallback.
2. **Alignment:** a similarity transform from five landmarks to the CelebA template (§6.3 of
   `PROJECT.md`); the crop is cut at the low resolution.
3. **Restoration** of all crops in batches, with PyTorch or ONNX Runtime.
4. **Paste-back:** a feathered ellipse, the inverse warp and a blend into the bicubic upscale of
   the whole image.
5. **Marking:** PNG text, XMP (IPTC digital source type `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`)
   and EXIF; container tags for videos. The record holds the model and its checksum, the
   parameters and the sha256 of the input. `--watermark` adds a visible mark.

In videos, face tracks smooth the landmarks between frames (PR 4.5).

## 5. Training data

- **Source:** CelebA aligned, from the Hugging Face mirror `eurecom-ds/celeba` (D8), with the
  official identity-disjoint split. Details and checksums: [`DATA.md`](DATA.md).
- **Train split:** 154,408 images of the official train partition after dropping the images
  annotated `Blurry` = 1 (D15).
- **Selection:** weights and recipes were chosen on the validation split (19,867 images); the
  test split was used only for the reported numbers.
- **HR targets:** 128×128 crops aligned to the template. Every crop is a downscale of the source
  (median ×0.82), never an upscale. Crops that reach outside the image (2.8% of train) use
  reflect padding (D16).
- **LR inputs:** made on the fly from the HR crop by a random degradation:
  - blur (isotropic and anisotropic Gaussian, motion, defocus);
  - resizing with a random interpolation, with and without antialias;
  - Gaussian or Poisson noise, colour or grey;
  - JPEG;
  - an optional second degradation stage, as in Real-ESRGAN;
  - grayscale with probability 0.02.

  The ×4 model trains on `configs/degrade/realistic.yaml`; the ×8 model on
  `configs/degrade/realistic_x8.yaml`, tuned against real 16 px faces (PR 2.6, D22).
- **Augmentation:** a random shift, rotation and scale of the crop (alignment jitter) and a
  horizontal flip.

**Known limits of the data** (`PROJECT.md` §6.4):
- celebrities, mostly near-frontal, well lit and made up, which real low-quality photos are not;
- every HR target carries JPEG compression artifacts;
- the only demographic annotations are CelebA's binary attributes (`Male`, `Young`, `Pale_Skin`
  and others); there is no skin-tone scale (D10).

## 6. Training procedure

Both models start from an L1 model of the same architecture (PR 3.1), then are fine-tuned for
2 epochs:

| | ×4 | ×8 |
|---|---|---|
| Start (L1, 30 epochs) | `srresnet_x4-20261125-093232`, Adam 2e-4, cosine decay | `srresnet_x8-20261125-095801`, the same |
| Fine-tuning losses | L1 1.0, identity 0.3, perceptual 0.03 | L1 1.0, identity 0.1 |
| Optimiser | Adam 1e-4, cosine decay to 1e-6, batch 16, bf16 | the same |
| Weights kept | exponential moving average (decay 0.999), last epoch | the same |
| Fine-tuning time | 503 s (RTX 6000 Ada) | 400 s |
| Config | `configs/train/srresnet_id_perc_robust_x4.yaml` | `configs/train/srresnet_id_robust_x8.yaml` |

- **Identity loss:** `1 − cos` of the facenet embeddings (Inception-ResNet-v1, VGGFace2
  weights) of the output and the target, on the central face square. It is made robust to pixel
  patterns: a random shift, rotation and scale of the square, and a Gaussian low-pass before the
  network (PR 3.4). Without these defences it raised the metric by drawing a periodic grid over
  the face (PR 3.3, D24).
- **Perceptual loss (×4 only):** VGG19 `conv5_4` features, with the same defences (PR 3.8).
  Without them it drew a fine hatching (PR 3.6, D27).
- **Weights selected on val** by rules set before the runs (D23): on light and medium inputs,
  better LPIPS and ID similarity than the previous reference, PSNR-Y at most 0.3 dB lower, and
  no subgroup losing more than 0.01 ID; a real-photo KID limit (D26); from PR 3.8 on, a spectral
  limit (D28).
- **Not used:** an adversarial loss. The ESRGAN recipe (PR 3.2) halved the ID similarity and
  invented features, and was rejected.
- **×8 and the perceptual term:** at ×8, every perceptual weight tried lowered ID similarity
  below the reference's, even with a higher identity weight (PR 3.9, 3.10).

## 7. Evaluation

### 7.1 Protocols

- **Synthetic test:** the frozen CelebA test split, 19,961 images of identities never seen in
  training, at four input levels:
  - `bicubic`: a clean bicubic downscale;
  - `light`, `medium`, `heavy`: blur, noise, JPEG and, for heavy, a second stage
    (`PROJECT.md` §9, PR 2.2).

  Each protocol's inputs are frozen by a checksum.
- **Metrics:**
  - PSNR-Y and SSIM-Y (luma, BT.601);
  - LPIPS (AlexNet);
  - **ID similarity:** the cosine similarity of ArcFace embeddings (InsightFace `buffalo_l`,
    D18) of the output and the original. This model is not the one the identity loss used.
    On CelebA val faces of 60 identities, same-person pairs score 0.565 on average and
    different people 0.015.
- **Real low-resolution photos:** WIDER FACE faces that are natively that small (D20), 8,645 at
  ×4 and 2,172 at ×8, with no reference. FID and KID against real CelebA faces.
- **End to end:** 1,000 synthetic scenes of CelebA **val** faces in a larger image, with
  detection, alignment and paste-back (PR 4.2).

### 7.2 Synthetic test

better, for LPIPS lower:

| light | 26.24 / 0.7486 / 0.4368 / 0.5465 | 27.83 / 0.8059 / 0.1795 / 0.4772 |
| medium | 24.57 / 0.6752 / 0.5483 / 0.3579 | 26.25 / 0.7503 / 0.2216 / 0.3128 |
| heavy | 22.83 / 0.6096 / 0.6305 / 0.1978 | 24.55 / 0.6912 / 0.2613 / 0.1965 |

**×8**

| Input level | Bicubic upscale | ×8 model |
|---|---|---|
| bicubic | 23.42 / 0.6339 / 0.5462 / 0.3160 | 25.37 / 0.7272 / 0.2617 / 0.3120 |
| light | 22.74 / 0.6082 / 0.6418 / 0.1920 | 24.39 / 0.6933 / 0.2938 / 0.1968 |
| medium | 21.38 / 0.5556 / 0.6889 / 0.0884 | 23.05 / 0.6449 / 0.3327 / 0.1191 |
| heavy | 19.89 / 0.5155 / 0.7162 / 0.0431 | 21.48 / 0.5894 / 0.3809 / 0.0733 |

- **Quality:** both models beat a bicubic upscale clearly on PSNR-Y, SSIM-Y and LPIPS at every
  level.
- **Identity, ×4:** below a bicubic upscale on every level, by 0.07 on light inputs and within
  0.002 on heavy ones. The model sharpens the face, but part of what it adds is not the person's.
- **Identity, ×8:** about equal to a bicubic upscale on clean inputs and above it on degraded
  ones. In absolute terms it is low: from 16 px the output face is mostly invented.
- **Artifact checks** (PR 3.7, 3.8): the spectral peak, which detects periodic patterns, is
  0.34 / 0.43 on light / medium at ×4 and 0.12 / 0.12 at ×8, below the gate limit of 1.0
  (D28). Models with a visible pattern scored 2.3 or more.

Observations:
- **`Male` = 0 has a lower ID similarity than `Male` = 1** everywhere, for the bicubic upscale
  too. Part of this gap therefore comes from the data or the recognition model, not from these
  models (PR 1.7).
- **Against the bicubic upscale, the models do worse on `Young` = 0 than on `Young` = 1** on
  every level at both scales. At ×4 the model minus bicubic is −0.079 against −0.067 on light
  inputs, and −0.028 against +0.008 on heavy ones. At ×8 it is −0.016 against +0.012 on light
  inputs. This fits what the evaluation grids show: the models make older people look younger.
- **Against the bicubic upscale, the models also do worse on `Male` = 1 than on `Male` = 0** on
  every level at both scales, by 0.005–0.032.
- **Uncertainty:** these are differences of means; no confidence intervals were computed, and
  the smallest of them (0.005–0.010) may be within noise.
- **The quality gate** limits the drop of each subgroup against the previous reference to 0.01
  (D23). It does not compare subgroups with each other.
- **Not measured:** skin tone (no annotation, D10), age beyond the binary `Young`, and any
  subgroup on real photos (WIDER FACE has no such labels).

### 7.4 End to end in a picture

1,000 CelebA val scenes per scale; degraded scenes add blur σ 0.5 px, noise σ 3 levels and
JPEG quality 70. ID similarity / LPIPS with detected faces (PR 4.2):

| Scale, input | Bicubic upscale of the image | Pipeline with the model |
|---|---|---|
| ×4 clean | 0.712 / 0.326 | 0.613 / 0.217 |
| ×4 degraded | 0.438 / 0.505 | 0.367 / 0.292 |
| ×8 clean | 0.340 / 0.544 | 0.261 / 0.347 |
| ×8 degraded | 0.129 / 0.684 | 0.128 / 0.421 |

- **Lower than the synthetic test:** cutting the face out at low resolution costs about 0.06 ID
  at both scales against crops on the template's grid. Fine-tuning on such crops did not
  recover it (PR 4.3), so it is mostly information lost in the resampling.
- **So in real use the models trade identity for quality:** better LPIPS, but an ID similarity
  below a bicubic upscale, except at ×8 on degraded faces, where both are equally low.
- **Detection:** the face was found in 99.6 / 99.0% (×4 clean / degraded) and 98.7 / 92.5% (×8)
  of these scenes, at the threshold 0.6 used then (now 0.8, D33).

### 7.5 Real low-resolution photos

FID / KID ×10³ against 19,867 real CelebA faces; lower means closer to the statistics of real
faces:

| Scale | Bicubic upscale | Model | Model, partly or heavily occluded faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| ×4 | 201.2 / 206.8 | 65.8 / 61.9 | KID 95.0 (1,720 faces) |
| ×8 | 317.3 / 379.4 | 105.0 / 98.4 | KID 121.1 (729 faces) |

These metrics reward a realistic face whether or not it is the right person, so they measure
realism, not faithfulness (PR 2.5). The evaluation grids (local, not measured) show both: real
faces become sharper, and at ×8 the model can draw a whole face from a dark blur.

### 7.6 Videos

Each frame is restored on its own. On synthetic clips of a still CelebA test face, moved and
degraded per frame, the restored faces flicker more than a bicubic upscale, even with the true
landmarks (mean absolute luma change between frames, 8-bit levels, PR 4.5):

| Scale, clip | Bicubic upscale | True landmarks | Detected and tracked |
|---|---|---|---|
| ×4 static, degraded | 3.12 | 3.51 | 3.71 |
| ×8 static, degraded | 3.53 | 4.55 | 4.84 |
| ×8 fast, degraded | 6.70 | 8.07 | 8.34 |

### 7.7 Speed

On a Ryzen 9 9950X with an RTX 6000 Ada, a small photo with one face takes 18 ms on the CPU and
12 ms on the GPU; a 1920 × 1080 frame with 8 faces takes 2.2–7.1 s, mostly face detection
(PR 5.5).

## 8. Limitations and risks

- **Invented details:** older people look younger, glasses can disappear, and at ×8 whole faces
  can be drawn from near-featureless inputs. Seen in the evaluation grids of these models and
  their L1 starts, not measured; §7.3 shows the age effect in the numbers.
- **Identity below a bicubic upscale** in real use (§7.4).
- **False faces:** on real WIDER FACE photos, 3% (×4) and 15% (×8) of the restored detections
  were not faces, and only 52% / 29% of the small faces were found. Both numbers are
  optimistic, because the detector was trained on those photos (D33).
- **Alignment errors:** the detector's landmarks often fall outside the training jitter, mostly
  in scale (D31).
- **Flicker** in videos (§7.6).
- **Narrow training data:** CelebA celebrities; real low-quality photos differ in pose,
  lighting, age range and skin tones.
- **The metrics themselves:** ID similarity comes from a recognition model trained on face
  photos, which may be biased across subgroups; FID and KID reward invention (§7.5).
- **Not measured:** landmark accuracy of the outputs (NME), skin tone, real videos.

## 10. Responsible use

- **Marking:** every output says it is AI-enhanced in its metadata; the API repeats it in an
  `X-Facehall-Notice` header (`CLAUDE.md` §11).
- **Privacy:** the API keeps uploads in memory only, and the logs hold no images, file names or
  face positions (PR 5.3). ONNX Runtime telemetry is off (PR 5.4).
- **Legal review:** *needs verification*. Photos of identifiable people are personal data, and
  face processing may fall under the rules on biometric data and on marking synthetic content
  (GDPR, the EU AI Act, the Ukrainian law on personal data protection). No legal review has been
  done; this card is not legal advice.

## 11. Reproducing

`PROJECT.md` §12 lists the commands. In short, with CelebA prepared (`DATA.md`):

```bash
uv run facehall train --config configs/train/srresnet_x4.yaml          # L1 start, ~27 min
uv run facehall train --config configs/train/srresnet_id_perc_robust_x4.yaml   # ~9 min
uv run facehall train --config configs/train/srresnet_x8.yaml          # L1 start, ~27 min
uv run facehall train --config configs/train/srresnet_id_robust_x8.yaml        # ~7 min
uv run facehall eval --config configs/eval/celeba128_light_x4.yaml --ckpt runs/<id>/best.safetensors
```

The `init` paths of the fine-tuning configs name the local L1 runs; point them at your own with
`--set train.init=runs/<id>/best.safetensors`. GPU runs are not bit-exact (cuDNN autotuning).
