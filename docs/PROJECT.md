### 6.3 Alignment and crop geometry

The geometry the alignment code (`facehall.align`, PR 1.3) builds on. Measured on 2026-11-24 over
the prepared manifest.

**Conventions**
- Landmarks and crops use pixel-centre coordinates (OpenCV). The CelebA landmark convention is
  not documented: the pixel-centre reading is an assumption, and its effect is at most 0.5 source
  px.
- The template uses normalized crop coordinates: (0, 0) is the top-left corner of the crop and
  (1, 1) its bottom-right corner. One template therefore serves every crop size (32, 128, 512).

**Template** (`facehall.align.CELEBA_TEMPLATE`)
- Derived from the train-split mean landmarks, so the test split is not used for design.
- Construction: the FFHQ alignment rule reduced to 5 points, a square of side
  max(4 × eye distance, 3.6 × eye-to-mouth distance).
- Then averaged with its mirror image, so that a horizontal flip commutes with alignment. This
  moves no point by more than 0.34 px at 128.
- Normalized (x, y): eyes (0.375, 0.47323) and (0.625, 0.47323), nose (0.5, 0.62947), mouth
  corners (0.388, 0.74094) and (0.612, 0.74094).
- On the mean train landmarks, the rule gives a square of side 153.1 px with x ∈ [12.0, 165.1] and
  y ∈ [38.7, 191.8], inside 178×218.

**Per-image alignment:** a least-squares similarity (Umeyama) maps the 5 landmarks to the
template.

| | train | val | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crop side in the source, median (min – max) | 155.8 px (143.4 – 256.9) | 155.9 px (144.4 – 234.7) | 156.0 px (39.3 – 247.8) |
| Crops whose HR 128 would be an upscale (side < 128) | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| Crops that need padding | 4,543 (2.79%) | 479 (2.41%) | 540 (2.71%) |
| Padded area of those crops: mean / p95 / max | 2.8% / 11.8% / 41.6% | 2.6% / 11.6% / 29.6% | 2.9% / 13.6% / 36.8% |
| Crops with more than 10% padding | 331 | 32 | 48 |

- **Upscale check:** so HR 128 is a downscale (×0.82 at the median) for every crop except the one
  test image with broken landmarks (`docs/DATA.md` §4.5). This confirms D4.
- **Large crops** come from profile and small faces: the 5 points fit the frontal template worse,
  and the face appears smaller in the crop.
- **Rotation** from the alignment is small, because the source is already aligned: median |angle|
  0.47°, p95 1.6°.
- **Cost:** decode 0.6 ms and align + warp 0.08 ms per image (single core).

| Scale | LR | HR | Interocular distance at the template |
|---|---|---|---|
| ×4 | 32×32 | 128×128 | 8 px in LR, 32 px in HR |
| ×8 | 16×16 | 128×128 | 4 px in LR, 32 px in HR |

### 6.4 Known data limitations

The limitations of the real-world set are listed in §6.5.

- All HR targets carry JPEG compression artifacts. Validation and test images are small JPEG files
  (≈6.8 KB each), and the train PNGs hold decoded JPEG pixels (`docs/DATA.md` §4.2). This is
  acceptable for 128 px but not for 512 px.
- Images with `Blurry = 1` (about 5% of every split) are poor HR targets. The manifest keeps them
  in the `attr_blurry` column. Whether to filter them is decided in PR 1.4 (Q4).
- There is no skin-tone annotation: `Pale_Skin` is a binary attribute, not a skin-tone scale.
  The plan is in D10.
- Some CelebA aligned images already contain stretched, streaky borders from the dataset's own
  alignment. Seen on 2026-11-24 in a train crop whose `padded` fraction was 0: the streaks come
  from the source image, not from our crop. How often this happens is not measured yet; `padded`
  (D16) covers only our crop reaching outside 178×218.
- Celebrity photos are not representative of real-world low-quality inputs; the Phase 2
  real-world test set addresses this.
- CelebA terms: non-commercial research only, no redistribution. They were verified on the
  official page and are quoted in `docs/THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md` §2. The mirror states no license
  of its own. Consequence: no CelebA images, annotations or manifests go into the public
  repository (D14, Q2).

### 6.5 Real-world set — summary

Details, counts, checksums and the evidence: `docs/DATA.md` §7. Decision and reasons: D20.

- **What:** faces from the WIDER FACE training photos whose native size already matches the
  model input. The alignment to the 32 px (×4) or 16 px (×8) template scales the source by
  0.8–1.25, so the inputs are not synthetic resizes. There is no HR reference.
- **Alignment:** the five RetinaFace landmarks, annotated manually and released only for the
  training images. Nothing here trains on WIDER FACE.
- **Prepared:** `/mnt/storage/AI/Face/wider_face/realworld/`, built by
  `scripts/prepare_wider.py` in about 10 s. It holds `x{4,8}/{val,test}/NNNNNN.png`,
  `manifest.parquet` and `prepare_info.json`.
- **Split:** by source image, 50/50; `val` for tuning, `test` frozen.

  | Scale | val | test |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | ×4 | 8,160 faces, 1,337 photos | 8,645 faces, 1,291 photos |
  | ×8 | 2,752 faces, 426 photos | 2,172 faces, 438 photos |

- **Stored per face:** the official attributes blur, expression, illumination, occlusion and pose,
  the alignment scale, angle and residual, and the padded fraction.
- **Limitations:**
  - no identities, so no ID similarity;
  - only 4–6% of the ×4 faces and under 1% of the ×8 faces are annotated as not blurred;
  - landmarks on faces with 3–5 px between the eyes are necessarily coarse, so some ×8 crops are
    misaligned;
  - by visual check on 48 samples per scale (not measured), the set covers a wider range of
    ages and skin tones than CelebA.
- **Terms:** WIDER FACE is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, the landmarks are non-commercial research only.
  Crops, the manifest and grids stay local.

## 9. Technical design

The agreed approach. Details are fixed in the corresponding PRs. Phase 1 first, then Phase 2.

### Phase 1

- **General.** PyTorch 2.x, YAML configs with OmegaConf, no magic constants in the code. Every run
  stores its config, git hash, seed and library versions in its own run directory (D17).
- **Dependencies (PR 1.1, done).** torch 2.15 / torchvision 0.29: the PyPI build (CUDA 13.2)
  locally and the CPU build in CI (§3). torch 2.15 CUDA wheels exist only for CUDA 13.2 (PyPI and
  the `cu132` index), so machines need a driver that supports CUDA 13.2 — check this on the 8 GB
  laptop. Also added: omegaconf, safetensors, pyarrow, opencv-python-headless (affine warps),
  torchmetrics[image]. MLflow moved to PR 1.6: it pulls in ~70 more packages (flask, sqlalchemy,
  docker, databricks-sdk, scikit-learn, …), so the tracking backend is chosen together with the
  trainer.
- **Data preparation (PR 1.2, done).** `scripts/prepare_dataset.py` wraps
  `facehall.data.celeba`.
  - Output: image files with the stored bytes (train PNG, val/test JPEG), `manifest.parquet` and
    `prepare_info.json`.
  - Before writing anything, it checks the shard set, the official counts and identity
    disjointness. Every image is decoded once.
  - Runtime about 2 min on this machine. Details: `docs/DATA.md`.
- **Alignment (PR 1.3, done).** `facehall.align` holds:
  - the template and its derivation (§6.3);
  - `umeyama` and matrix helpers;
  - `alignment_matrix(landmarks, template, size)`;
  - `warp`: bicubic by default; border reflect, constant or replicate;
  - `unwarp`, the inverse warp for paste-back in Phase 4;
  - `valid_mask`, which marks padded pixels;
  - `Jitter`: a random similarity about the crop centre, with shift, rotation and log-uniform
    scale. Its limits are set in the PR 1.4 config. Every sample draws four numbers, so the random
    stream does not depend on the settings.
- **Dataset + degradation v0 (PR 1.4, done).** `facehall.data.dataset.AlignedFaces(data, degrade,
  split, scale, seed=…)`, configured by `configs/data/celeba_128.yaml` and
  `configs/degrade/bicubic.yaml` (`facehall.config.load_config`).
  - Data root: `${FACEHALL_DATA:-/mnt/storage/AI/Face}/celeba`.
  - Dropped samples:
    - HR crops that would be upscales (`CLAUDE.md` §7): one test image;
    - `Blurry = 1` images in train (D15).
  - Resulting sizes: train 154,408, val 19,867, test 19,961 (`docs/DATA.md` §3).
  - Each sample:
    - the source is warped once with the alignment matrix, composed in train with jitter and a
      horizontal flip;
    - padding is reflect (D16);
    - LR is the degradation of the HR crop;
    - output: `hr` (3×128×128) and `lr` (3×32×32 or 3×16×16), both float32 in [0, 1] on 8-bit
      levels, plus `index`, `padded` and `blurry`.
  - Degradation v0: torch bicubic with antialias, then 8-bit quantization. It matches PIL
    `BICUBIC` within 1 level; without antialias the difference reaches 142 levels. Each run
    records the exact function (`describe()`).
  - Randomness: a per-sample generator seeded with `(seed, epoch, index)` in train (call
    `set_epoch`) and `(seed, index)` in val/test, so samples do not depend on workers or order.
  - Validation and test crops are computed on the fly, deterministically, not cached (see
    `DECISIONS.md`).
  - Throughput: 909 samples/s single-process. With 8 / 16 DataLoader workers it is 4,200 / 9,900
    samples/s, and 7,700 / 13,100 samples/s when `cv2.setNumThreads(1)` is called in the main
    process first. That is 0.2–0.6 min per train epoch, so data loading is not the bottleneck.
- **Models (PR 1.5, done).** `facehall.models.build_model(config, scale, lr_size)` with
  `configs/model/{bicubic,fnet,cnet}.yaml`. Input and output are (B, 3, h, w) in [0, 1].
  - **Baseline:** `bicubic` (torch bicubic upsampling, clamped).
  - **Ports:** FNet and CNet, layer for layer as in `FH.ipynb`, including BN, the sigmoid output
    and the Keras defaults (Glorot init, zero bias, BN eps 1e-3 / momentum 0.99, PReLU alpha 0).
    `scale` is generalised as one ×2 stage per factor of 2.
  - **Parameter counts:** equal to the notebook's `model.summary()` outputs at 24 px ×4:
    - FNet: 734,723 trainable + 1,408 BN statistics;
    - CNet: 854,979 + 384.
  - **CNet's `PReLU()`** has no `shared_axes`, so it learns one alpha per channel *and* pixel. The
    port keeps this, so CNet accepts only its LR size, and its size grows with it: 1,457,091
    parameters at 32 px ×4, 1,510,531 at 16 px ×8. FNet has 734,723 parameters at ×4 and 882,499
    at ×8.
  - **Legacy Keras weights** cannot be loaded as they are: the layout is NCHW, and PyTorch's
    pixel-shuffle channel order differs from `tf.nn.depth_to_space`. They are not needed (§5).
  - **Training step on the RTX 6000 Ada** (Adam, MSE, 128 px HR):

    | Model | Batch | bf16 | fp32 | Peak memory (bf16 / fp32) |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | FNet ×4 | 16 / 64 | 3,800 / 3,700 img/s | 2,000 / 1,800 img/s | 0.3 / 1.2 GiB; 2.4 / 3.7 GiB |
    | FNet ×8 | 16 / 64 | 4,100 / 4,200 img/s | 2,200 / 2,000 img/s | 0.2 / 0.7 GiB; 0.4 / 1.4 GiB |
    | CNet ×4 | 16 / 64 | 4,000 / 3,000 img/s | 2,800 / 2,400 img/s | 0.5 / 1.9 GiB; 2.7 / 5.4 GiB |
    | CNet ×8 | 16 / 64 | 3,900 / 3,000 img/s | 2,700 / 2,400 img/s | 0.4 / 1.5 GiB; 0.5 / 1.9 GiB |

    So every configuration fits 8 GB (D6), and one train epoch (154,408 samples) takes about
    40–50 s in bf16 on this GPU.
  - **Legacy training recipe** (for the PR 1.6 configs):
    - inputs in [0, 1], MSE loss;
    - Adam with Keras defaults (lr 1e-3, β 0.9 / 0.999, ε 1e-7), batch 16;
    - FNet: 10 epochs with early stopping (patience 3 on val loss);
    - CNet: 50 epochs.
- **Trainer + CLI (PR 1.6, done).** `facehall train --config configs/train/<name>.yaml [--smoke]
  [--set key=value …]`, implemented by `facehall.train.train(config)`.
  - **Configs:** `configs/train/{fnet,cnet}_x{4,8}.yaml` carry the legacy recipe (see "Legacy
    training recipe" above). They pull in the data, degradation and model configs through
    `include`, and `--set` overrides any value.
  - **No separate 48 GB profile:** all four configs fit 8 GB at the recipe batch size of 16 (peak
    ≤ 2.8 GiB even in fp32, table above). The batch size is part of the recipe, so a larger one
    is an experiment (`--set train.batch_size=64`), not a profile (D6).
  - **Run directory** `runs/<name>-<YYYYmmdd-HHMMSS>[-smoke]/` (D17):
    - `config.yaml`, the resolved config;
    - `run.json`, provenance: command, git commit, library versions, device, dataset settings and
      counts, and the manifest checksum;
    - `metrics.jsonl`, one record per logged step and per epoch;
    - `train.log`, `summary.json`;
    - checkpoints `last` and `best` (lowest val loss), each a `.safetensors` file with a `.json`
      file next to it. The JSON holds the full config and the sha256 of the weights;
      `load_checkpoint` rebuilds the model from these two files alone.
  - **Training:**
    - Adam;
    - bf16 autocast for the forward pass, with the loss and validation in fp32;
    - gradient accumulation (`train.grad_accum`);
    - early stopping on val loss (`train.early_stopping`);
    - optional EMA of the weights (`train.ema_decay`). With EMA, validation and `best` use the
      EMA weights, and `last_ema` is saved too.
  - **Validation:** after every epoch, on the full val split. It reports the mean per-image
    loss and an RGB PSNR, which is only a monitoring value; the evaluation protocol is PR 1.7.
  - **Reproducibility:**
    - the seed fixes model initialisation, sample order (a permutation per `(seed, epoch)`) and
      augmentation;
    - on CPU, two runs with one seed give identical weights (tested);
    - on GPU, cuDNN autotuning allows small differences.
  - **DataLoader notes,** verified 2026-11-24:
    - call `cv2.setNumThreads(1)` in the main process before the workers start; the trainer does
      this;
    - **never call it inside a forked worker**: once the parent has used OpenCV's thread pool,
      that call deadlocks;
    - workers are not persistent, so each epoch's workers see `set_epoch`.
  - **Smoke runs on the real data,** 2026-11-24 (RTX 6000 Ada, bf16, 8 workers):
    - **Default `--smoke`** (512 train / 256 val samples, 2 epochs): about 4 s per config, all
      four configs.
    - **Timing runs** (40,000 train samples, full val, 2 epochs): 3,600–3,800 train img/s and
      3.2–3.5 s per validation pass for every config, so a full epoch takes about 45–47 s.
    - **Estimated full runs:** FNet ×4 / ×8 at most 10 epochs, about 8 min each; CNet ×4 / ×8
      50 epochs, about 39 min each. About 1.6 h in total when run one after another.
    - **Early val loss:** in the 32-step default smoke runs, val loss is 1.7–6.8× the train loss. In
      the 2,500-step timing runs it is already below the train loss, which is computed on
      augmented samples. This is consistent with BN running statistics that follow slowly
      (Keras momentum 0.99), but the cause was not checked separately.
  - **Full runs,** 2026-11-24, from commit `9dcc51e` in a separate worktree, so the git hash
    in every run is clean. Run directories are in `runs/` (local only):

    | Run | Epochs | Time | Best epoch (val loss) |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `fnet_x4-20261124-101949` | 10 | 8.4 min | 10 |
    | `fnet_x8-20261124-102817` | 10 | 9.4 min | 10 |
    | `cnet_x4-20261124-103744` | 50 | 38.2 min | 44 |
    | `cnet_x8-20261124-111557` | 50 | 38.8 min | 47 |

    - FNet's val loss still fell in its last epoch, so the 10 epochs of the legacy recipe
      probably stop it early.
    - Some epochs took about 70 s instead of about 46 s while an evaluation ran on the same CPU.
  - **Resuming** from `last` was added in PR 3.1 (`facehall train --resume`, §9 Phase 3).
- **Evaluation (PR 1.7, done).** `facehall eval --config configs/eval/celeba128_x{4,8}.yaml
  (--ckpt runs/<id>/best.safetensors | --model bicubic)`; protocol details in `DECISIONS.md`
  (2026-11-24, "Evaluation protocol").
  - **Protocol:** the config fixes split, data, degradation, seed and metrics; the checkpoint
    gives only the model, and its scale and HR size are checked against the protocol.
  - **Metrics, per image:** PSNR-Y and SSIM-Y (BT.601, no border crop), LPIPS (AlexNet), ID
    similarity. SR is rounded to 8 bit first.
  - **ID similarity:** InsightFace `buffalo_l` `w600k_r50.onnx` (D18) on CPU through ONNX
    Runtime. Crops go to the ArcFace template with one fixed similarity, so no detector is
    involved. HR embeddings are cached once per fingerprint, so protocols with the same HR
    crops share them (`outputs/eval/_cache/`, PR 2.2; per protocol before).
    Sanity check on 60 val identities: same-identity pairs 0.565 mean cosine, different
    identities 0.015 (max 0.143).
  - **Subsets:** all, `Blurry` = 0 (D15), no padding (D16), `Male` = 1 / 0, `Young` = 1 / 0.
  - **Outputs:** `outputs/eval/<protocol>/<split>/<model>/` holds `metrics.json` (means, standard
    deviations, provenance) and `per_image.parquet`. Grid samples stay local (D19).
  - **Report:** `scripts/make_results.py` writes `docs/RESULTS.md` from the `metrics.json` files and
    the comparison grids to `outputs/results/`.
  - **Weights:** `scripts/download_weights.py buffalo_l` (size and sha256 pinned in
    `facehall.weights`). LPIPS downloads the torchvision AlexNet weights on first use (D11).
  - NME moves to Phase 3, because it needs a landmark model.
  - **Phase 1 evaluation,** 2026-11-24: from commit `014fa61` in a separate worktree. Each
    protocol took about 4.5 min for bicubic (including the HR embeddings) and 2.4 min per trained
    model. The tables are in [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). Observations:
    - Both trained models beat bicubic on PSNR-Y, SSIM-Y and LPIPS. Only FNet also improves ID
      similarity; CNet's is about equal to bicubic's (slightly lower at ×4).
    - At ×8, ID similarity is low for every model, as expected (§11). In the local ×8 grid, the
      SR faces look smoothed and average, and older faces look younger. This was seen on 8
      samples and not measured.
    - ID similarity is lower for `Male` = 0 than for `Male` = 1 for every model, including
      bicubic. Part of the gap therefore comes from the data or the recognition model, not the SR
      model. Phase 3 subgroup gates must compare models against this baseline gap.
- **Reproduction.** `scripts/reproduce_phase1.sh` runs prepare → train (FNet, CNet × ×4, ×8) →
  eval → `RESULTS.md`.

### Phase 2

- **Degradation pipeline (PR 2.1, done).** `kind: pipeline` in `DegradeConfig`
  (`facehall.data.degrade.Pipeline`); details and reasons in `DECISIONS.md` (2026-11-24,
  "Degradation pipeline"). The training mix is `configs/degrade/realistic.yaml`.
  - **Per sample:**
    1. grayscale (probability 0.02 in `realistic`), which turns the HR target grey too;
    2. stage `first`: blur → resize → noise → JPEG;
    3. optional stage `second` (Real-ESRGAN-style second order): the first stage then ends at
       0.75–2× the LR size, and the second one resizes to the LR size;
    4. optional colour shift of the LR only (off in `realistic`);
    5. clamp and round to 8-bit levels.
  - **Operations:**
    - blur: isotropic and anisotropic Gaussian, motion, defocus (`facehall.data.kernels`);
    - resize: bicubic, bilinear, area, nearest, with antialias drawn for bicubic and bilinear;
    - noise: Gaussian, Poisson, colour or grey;
    - JPEG: OpenCV, single-channel for grey images.
  - **Units:** blur sizes are in LR pixels and are scaled to each stage's working resolution, so
    one config gives the same blur in the LR image at ×4 and ×8. Noise is in 8-bit levels.
  - **Interface:** a degradation returns `Degraded(lr, hr, params)`. Each sample carries the
    drawn parameters as a JSON string (`degradation`); evaluation stores them per image
    (PR 2.2).
  - `kind: bicubic` (v0) is unchanged. The pipeline with default settings reproduces it exactly
    (tested).
  - **HR embedding cache:** the fingerprint includes the degradation only when it can change the
    HR target (grayscale), so the Phase 1 caches stay valid.
  - **Not implemented:** H.264/H.265 (optional in `CLAUDE.md` §7) and Real-ESRGAN's sinc filter.
  - **Measured on 2026-11-24** (CelebA train, 8 DataLoader workers):
    - one `realistic` degradation call takes about 0.6 ms;
    - loading runs at about 5,000 samples/s with `realistic`, against 7,300–7,500 with bicubic;
      both are above the ~3,700 img/s of training;
    - smoke runs of FNet ×4 and ×8 with `realistic` pass.
  - **Preview:** `scripts/preview_degradations.py` writes a grid of HR crops and LR variants and
    the drawn parameters to `outputs/degrade/` (local, D19). In the first look, `realistic` leans
    towards heavy blur, so the first-stage maximum sigma was lowered from 1.2 to 1.0 LR px. The
    ranges are initial values, to be tuned against the real-world test set.
- **Synthetic test levels (PR 2.2, done).** Details and reasons in `DECISIONS.md`
  (2026-11-24, "Synthetic test levels").
  - **Levels:** `configs/degrade/levels/{light,medium,heavy}.yaml`. Every sample is blurred,
    resized, noised and JPEG-compressed:

    | Level | Blur σ (LR px) | Motion / defocus (LR px) | Noise σ (levels) | JPEG quality | Second stage |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | light | 0.1–0.4 | 0.5–1.0 / 0.3–0.6 | 1–3 | 75–95 | no |
    | medium | 0.4–0.7 | 1.0–2.0 / 0.6–1.0 | 3–7 | 50–75 | no |
    | heavy | 0.7–1.0 | 2.0–3.0 / 1.0–1.5 | 7–15 | 30–50 | always |

    - Ranges do not overlap between levels and are no stronger than the training mix
      (`realistic`), which is tested.
    - Poisson noise uses the peak that gives the same noise at mid-grey as the Gaussian range.
    - The resize mix is the training mix.
    - No grayscale.
  - **Protocols:** `configs/eval/celeba128_{light,medium,heavy}_x{4,8}.yaml`, plus the two
    bicubic protocols from Phase 1 (`level: bicubic`). All on the frozen test split, seed 0.
  - **Frozen inputs:**
    - every evaluation records the sha256 of all LR and HR crops (`inputs_sha256` in
      `metrics.json`); the digest does not depend on the batch size or the workers;
    - all eight protocols list their digest, so an evaluation whose inputs differ fails instead
      of producing numbers that cannot be compared (for example after an OpenCV or numpy
      update);
    - `scripts/protocol_digest.py` prints and checks the digests. Computing all eight takes about
      30 s.
  - **Per image:** `per_image.parquet` stores the drawn degradation parameters (`degradation`,
    JSON), so results can be broken down by blur, noise or JPEG without re-running.
  - **Report:** `RESULTS.md` starts with a summary per scale (model × level). It fails if the
    models of one protocol were scored on different inputs.
  - **Evaluation of the Phase 1 models,** 2026-11-24:
    - bicubic, FNet and CNet on all eight protocols, from commit `8f0c0af` in a separate
      worktree; 24 runs in 71 min;
    - the HR embeddings were computed once and shared by all protocols;
    - every input digest matched its frozen value;
    - the bicubic-protocol tables are identical to the Phase 1 ones, so the Phase 1 results
      reproduce from this commit.
    - Tables: [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). Observations:

    - XMP with the IPTC digital source type `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`;
    - EXIF.

    The record holds the model and its sha256, the parameters, the input sha256 and the
    restored regions. It holds no file names, landmarks or image content. C2PA is not written
    (local use, D13).
  - **Paste-back** blends in uint8 frames, a window per face. Single-face outputs are
    unchanged; overlapping faces may differ by 1 level.
  - **Face-size limits:** a face more than 2× larger (`max_downscale`, PR 4.2) or 2× smaller
    (`max_upscale`, new) than the model input is left to the bicubic background.
  - **False faces on real photos** (`measure_detection.py --parts wider`, D33): on 200 WIDER
    `val` photos, against the official boxes, detections that the pipeline would restore:

    | Threshold | ×4: faces / false per photo / faces found | ×8: faces / false per photo / faces found |
    |---|---|---|
    | 0.6 (D31) | 0.84 / 0.98 / 0.69 | 0.51 / 2.88 / 0.51 |
    | 0.8 (D33) | 0.97 / 0.12 / 0.52 | 0.85 / 0.32 / 0.29 |

    - Optimistic: YuNet was trained on these photos.
    - At 0.6 the model drew faces on ties, buttons and printed letters (seen in two group
      photos), so the pipeline now uses 0.8 (D33).
    - On synthetic CelebA scenes 0.8 costs little: ×8 degraded 0.950 → 0.900 detected, ×4
      0.993 → 0.987.
    - A team photo at ×4: 66 faces restored, all real (seen, not measured).
- **Videos (PR 4.5, in review).** Interface, measurements, selection rules and reasons in
  `DECISIONS.md` ("Video: frame by frame, face tracks and the flicker measure (PR 4.5)").
  - **Command:** `facehall enhance` takes video files (`.mp4 .mov .mkv .avi .webm .m4v`) like
    images, also in folders. The output is MP4 by default (`--video-format mkv|mov`).
  - **Processing:** each frame is restored on its own by the pipeline.
    - **Reading:** OpenCV, with any stored rotation applied.
    - **Encoding:** the system `ffmpeg` (H.264, CRF 18). The pip OpenCV has no H.264, audio or
      tags.
    - **Audio:** the input's is kept (AAC in MP4 and MOV).
    - **Marks:** container tags (`ai_enhanced`, the notice, the IPTC digital source type and the
      record with face counts).
  - **Face tracks** (`facehall.video.FaceTracker`, `video` in `configs/pipeline/default.yaml`):
    - an alpha-beta filter of each face's landmarks and box (alpha 0.5, beta 0.1);
    - hysteresis: a track begins at the pipeline's threshold 0.8 (D33) and continues with
      detections down to 0.3;
    - no face is restored in a frame without a detection there (`hold` 0).
  - **Flicker measure** (`facehall.eval.temporal`, `scripts/measure_temporal.py`): synthetic
    CelebA clips (a still face, moved and degraded per frame). The measure is the mean absolute
    luma change between consecutive aligned face crops, in 8-bit levels. The settings were
    chosen on 100 val clips by two rules set before the runs. Test, flicker / restored fraction:

    | Scale, condition | bicubic | true landmarks | per-frame detection | tracked |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | ×4 static, degraded | 3.12 | 3.51 | 4.03 / 0.992 | 3.71 / 0.995 |
    | ×4 fast, degraded | 5.33 | 5.62 | 5.85 / 0.991 | 5.71 / 0.993 |
    | ×8 static, degraded | 3.53 | 4.55 | 5.75 / 0.869 | 4.84 / 0.922 |
    | ×8 drift, degraded | 5.79 | 7.11 | 8.07 / 0.873 | 7.41 / 0.956 |
    | ×8 fast, degraded | 6.70 | 8.07 | 8.97 / 0.868 | 8.34 / 0.949 |

  - **Observations:**
    - **Tracking:** it removes most of the flicker that detection jitter adds, and restores the
      face in more frames, at equal or better PSNR-Y.
    - **The model itself:** even with true landmarks, the restored faces flicker more than a
      bicubic upscale. Each frame is restored on its own.
    - **Strong smoothing** (alpha 0.3 or less, or no velocity) trailed fast faces. The first
      rule's winner failed the added fast-motion check (`DECISIONS.md`).
    - **Hold, rejected for safety:** restoring a face for a few frames without any detection
      flickered about as little, but it would draw faces after cuts. Hysteresis also restored
      more faces.
  - **Speed** (RTX 6000 Ada, about 62 faces per frame): 640 × 360 at ×4 runs at 8.3 frames/s
    end to end, and 320 × 180 at ×8 at 8.6 frames/s; peak memory 2.4 GB.

### Risks

- False face detections on real photos make the model draw faces on textures (ties, prints).
  At the pipeline threshold 0.8 (D33), on WIDER photos, 3% of the restorable detections at ×4
  and 15% at ×8 are still not faces, and only 52% / 29% of the small faces are found. These
  numbers are optimistic, because YuNet was trained on those photos. Visual checks of outputs
  remain necessary.
- The face detector's alignment error often exceeds the training jitter, mostly in scale: only
  31–63% of detected faces fall inside it on synthetic CelebA scenes (D31), and real photos are
  harder. On those scenes it costs little ID (PR 4.2: −0.002 to −0.012 against annotated
  landmarks); real photos may cost more.
- The evaluation protocols cut the LR crop on the template's pixel grid, which no real photo
  gives. In the pipeline the alignment at LR resolution costs about 0.06 ID at both scales
  (PR 4.2), so the `RESULTS.md` numbers overstate the product in ID. On clean and lightly
  degraded faces, the pipeline's ID is below a plain bicubic upscale. Fine-tuning on such crops
  does not recover it (PR 4.3).
- ×8 from 16 px means ≈4 px between the eyes: the output is mostly invented, and ID similarity
  will be low. This must be stated clearly (`CLAUDE.md` §11).
- JPEG artifacts in the HR targets (§6.4) limit perceptual quality.
- The licensing of the InsightFace recognition model is not fully clear (D18). If InsightFace
  requires a separate license for research use, the ID-similarity metric needs another model, and
  the published ID numbers must be recomputed.
- ID similarity is computed with a model that was itself trained on face photos; it measures
  closeness to the reference, not identity "recovery", and it may be biased across subgroups.
- CI time: about 40 s with the CPU build of torch (PR #4); watch it as the tests grow.
- DataLoader workers use `fork`, the Linux default. Python 3.12 warns that forking a
  multi-threaded process may deadlock (seen in the tests). The one deadlock found so far is the
  OpenCV one (§9), and the trainer avoids it. Python 3.14 changes the default to `forkserver`;
  revisit this when moving to it.
- The realistic degradation ranges were tuned against real LR faces in PR 2.6, but only by
  feature statistics (KID/FID), which also react to content. At ×8 the tuned mix allows strong
  noise (σ ≤ 35), part of which may stand for background clutter rather than camera noise (§9).
  The tuned ×8 mix also lies outside the medium and heavy synthetic levels in blur and JPEG, so
  a model trained on it is tested out of range there.
- MSE-trained models on heavy inputs invent generic faces: they change apparent age and drop
  accessories such as glasses (seen in PR 2.4 grids, not measured). Report this in the model
  card and the UI (`CLAUDE.md` §11). Any identity gate in Phase 3 should be checked on such
  cases, not only on means. PR 2.5 grids show the same on real photos: at ×8 the
  realistic-trained model invents whole faces from near-featureless inputs.
- FID and KID on the real-world set use CelebA as the reference, so they reward CelebA-like,
  invented faces as much as faithful ones (PR 2.5). They must not be the only real-world gate.
- A face box (`--box`) without a detection inside it makes the model draw a face wherever the
  box is, even on background. The box landmarks assume YuNet's tight boxes: a looser hand-drawn
  box gives a face too large for the model, which the face-size limit then leaves alone (PR 4.4).
- `facehall enhance` reads the reference checkpoints from the local `runs/` folder (or
  `FACEHALL_RUNS`). The weights are not published (D7, D13), so on another machine the command
  needs `--ckpt` or a copy of the runs.
- Restored faces in videos flicker more than a bicubic upscale, even with perfect landmarks
  (PR 4.5). Each frame is restored on its own, and the model amplifies the noise, compression
  and sub-pixel changes between frames. Face tracking removes only the flicker that detection
  jitter adds. A temporal model or temporal blending would be needed for more.
- The flicker measure uses synthetic clips: a still CelebA face that is moved and degraded per
  frame. There is no real video set. Expressions, head turns, lighting changes, occlusion and
  scene cuts are not measured. There is no cut detection: a track continues across a cut only
  if a detection overlaps its predicted box there.
- Videos need the system `ffmpeg`; tests that encode video are skipped without it. Inputs with
  a variable frame rate are written at OpenCV's frame rate, which is not verified (PR 4.5).
- The mirror has no checksum from the original CelebA release. Integrity rests on the row and
  identity counts. The sha256 of every shard is recorded in `docs/DATA.md` §2, so later changes to
  the local copy are detectable.
